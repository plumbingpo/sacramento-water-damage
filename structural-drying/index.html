Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Sacramento Restoration Co are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

What to Expect From Our Structural Drying

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

Hygrometers, moisture meters and a thermal camera let us map moisture across each area that was affected. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

Final readings are documented, the equipment comes out and your property is certified at dry standard as soon as all affected materials test at acceptable levels.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.
