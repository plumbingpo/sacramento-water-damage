First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Our Process

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
