Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Repair is a different job. Think of it as all the protective work that comes first, aimed at saving what remains sound. Our Sacramento crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. Each is its own phase of the job. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

When to Call for Water Damage Mitigation in Sacramento

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The leak is fixed, yet cabinets, walls or flooring remain wet.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • Water is moving from one room into the next along the flooring.
  • Your insurer has told you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.
  • A plumber fixed the pipe but nobody has dealt with the water.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.

What a Professional Brings to Water Damage Mitigation

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Is removing the cabinet toe kick necessary? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. That file shows what the water did and what was done about it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Never switch the furnace fully off in freezing weather, since pipes can freeze and split.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.

How We Handle Water Damage Mitigation

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Extract the Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Plastic barriers and controlled airflow separate wet rooms from dry ones. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Moisture readings are recorded on every visit until the structure can be repaired, typically in 3 to 5 days.
