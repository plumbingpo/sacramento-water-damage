Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Sacramento

Think of drywall as a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Warning Signs

  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Water Next Door

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Wet Sheathing Outside

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Growth Behind the Paint

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Insulation Holding Water

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.
