Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. For flood emergencies anywhere in Sacramento, Sacramento Restoration Co is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. After that, IICRC-certified technicians on our team measure how contaminated the area is, then sanitize it with antimicrobial products, HEPA-filtered air cleaning and targeted disinfection of all surfaces the water reached. Materials beyond saving, like contaminated drywall, damaged insulation and saturated carpet pad, are removed and disposed of the right way.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

Signs It Is Time for Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • Storm damage that lets rain in through the roof or walls
  • A sewer or drain backup pushing contaminated water into living spaces
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • Soft, wet drywall or carpeting that is soaked through
  • Standing water anywhere on your property, whatever the source

Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Check that grading on all sides of the house carries water away from the foundation
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • A battery-powered backup sump pump keeps water moving if the electricity fails in a storm
  • Wherever flooding is likely, get appliances and electrical equipment up above floor level
