Wet Carpet Drying: What Sacramento Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. Our team in Sacramento extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

What It Looks Like

  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Swollen Subfloor

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

Furniture Marks

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.
