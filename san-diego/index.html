Where Water Damage Starts in San Diego Properties

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for San Diego households. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in San Diego. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many San Diego homes is well along before a stain appears. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.

Working With Your Insurer After Water Damage in San Diego

Adjusters work from evidence, so the strongest thing a San Diego homeowner can bring to a water damage claim is a good record. Start building it before anything is cleaned up or thrown away.

Get pictures of whatever failed, whether a hose, a valve or a pipe joint, and hang on to the part itself. Shoot every room from the doorway, then move in for close-ups of ruined belongings and the model numbers on electrical goods. Do not discard damaged carpet, furniture or contents until the adjuster has seen them or agreed to photos. Keep receipts for anything you spend because of the loss, from fans to a hotel stay.

Insurers often draw a line between sudden damage, like a burst pipe, and damage from a slow leak or outside flooding. Simple Ways to Keep a San Diego Home Dry

Most of the water damage we see in San Diego homes started with something small that gave warning first. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Start with supply lines. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. A stain that spreads or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, and San Diego homeowners are welcome to ask us about it.
